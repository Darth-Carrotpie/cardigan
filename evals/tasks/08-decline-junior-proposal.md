# 08 · Saying no to a junior's proposal

- **Kind:** chat, team management
- **Right size:** short but warm (curt is a failure)
- **Tests:** politeness as a never-cut item, writing downward, decision clarity

## Prompt

```
Mia (junior designer, four months in) sent me a two-page proposal to rebuild our icon set in-house. It's genuinely good thinking – she audited all 140 icons and found 31 inconsistent ones – but we're not doing it this quarter: no capacity, and we're evaluating a vendor icon library in January. Reply to her on Slack. I'm her team lead.
```

## Good outcome

A few warm sentences. One specific thing praised (the audit, 31 inconsistent icons out of 140). A clear decision: not this quarter. The reason: capacity, plus the vendor evaluation in January. A next step that keeps her involved, such as bringing her audit into the January vendor evaluation. It should be short, but it must not read as a brush-off from her boss.

## Checks (pass/fail)

1. **Decision (never-cut):** clearly not this quarter; no false hope of "maybe next week".
2. **Reason (never-cut):** capacity and/or the January vendor evaluation.
3. **Specific praise:** names something concrete from her work (the audit, 31/140), not just "great work".
4. **Next step:** offers a way forward (involve her in the January evaluation, revisit then).
5. **Tone (judge):** a junior designer would not read it as dismissive.
6. **Numbers:** if mentioned, 140 icons and 31 inconsistent are correct.

## Lighter option

None. A one-line "not this quarter, thanks" is the failure this task looks for.

## Measure

As task 01.
