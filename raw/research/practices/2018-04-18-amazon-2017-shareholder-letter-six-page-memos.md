# 2017 Letter to Shareholders

> Source: https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2017-letter-to-shareholders
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2018-04-18 (page shows no date; letter covering 2017 released April 2018)
> Author: Jeff Bezos / Amazon
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> We don't do PowerPoint (or any other slide-oriented) presentations at Amazon.

> narratively structured six-page memos

> study hall

> Not surprisingly, the quality of these memos varies widely.

> I believe high standards are teachable.

> High standards are contagious.

> it takes about six months of daily practice.

> The great memos are written and re-written, shared with colleagues who are asked to improve the work

> simply can't be done in a day or two.

> a great memo probably should take a week or more.

> you can improve results through the simple act of teaching scope

## Collector's note

Bezos's own public account of Amazon's no-slides, six-page narrative memo practice, which is read silently at the start of meetings ("study hall"). The letter's point is that a great memo takes a week or more of drafting and peer revision, so people should be taught the real scope of the work. For cardigan this is the counterweight to "produce less". For decisions that matter, the leaner form may be one well-reasoned narrative that replaces a deck and a meeting's worth of talk, not a shorter text. Fewer artifacts, not necessarily fewer words. Fetch-tool limits mean only short verbatim fragments are kept.
