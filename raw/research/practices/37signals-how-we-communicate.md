# Guide to Internal Communication, the Basecamp Way (How we communicate)

> Source: https://37signals.com/how-we-communicate
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: Unknown
> Author: Jason Fried / 37signals (Basecamp)
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> You can not not communicate.

> Real-time sometimes, asynchronous most of the time.

> Give meaningful discussions a meaningful amount of time to develop and unfold.

> Meetings are the last resort, not the first option.

> Writing solidifies, chat dissolves.

> Speaking only helps who's in the room, writing helps everyone.

> The expectation of immediate response is toxic.

> Poor communication creates more work.

> Companies don't have communication problems, they have miscommunication problems.

> Communication shouldn't require schedule synchronization.

> 'Now' is often the wrong time to say what just popped into your head.

> If you want an answer, you have to ask a question.

> Urgency is overrated, ASAP is poison.

> Where you put something, and what you call it, matters.

> Time is on your side, rushing makes conversations worse.

> Communication is lossy, especially verbal communication.

> Ask if things are clear.

> Saying the right thing at the right time in the right way with the fewest side effects.

## Collector's note

A list of about 30 internal communication principles from 37signals, the Basecamp company, written by Jason Fried. Fetched excerpts are short verbatim lines. Other guidelines, such as adding factual and spatial context to head off "wait, what?" questions, deleting passages to test whether they mattered, and not pulling people into work off-hours, were seen only as paraphrase and are not quoted here. For cardigan it gives a practitioner basis for "async by default, meeting last", "say it later rather than now", "ask an explicit question if you want an answer" and "where you put it matters" (link or thread over a new message). It also reminds us that over-cutting brings back miscommunication cost ("poor communication creates more work").
