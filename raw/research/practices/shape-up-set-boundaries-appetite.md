## Excerpts

> The amount of time we want to spend on a project, as opposed to an estimate.

> You can think of the appetite as a time budget for a standard team size.

> This is a project that a team of one designer and one or two programmers can build in one or two weeks.

> This project takes the same-size team a full six-weeks.

> This principle, called "fixed time, variable scope," is key to successfully defining and shipping projects.

> There's no absolute definition of "the best" solution. The best is relative to your constraints.

> Our default response to any idea that comes up should be: "Interesting. Maybe some day."

## Collector's note

The "appetite" chapter of Basecamp's Shape Up book. Its method is to fix the time budget first and flex scope to fit, rather than estimate a fixed scope. It also sets a default soft "no" for raw ideas. For cardigan this carries over to light planning: state the appetite ("this is worth a 1-page note / 30 minutes"), cut scope rather than stretch effort, and treat a polite "Interesting. Maybe some day." as an acceptable full reply to an unrequested idea.
