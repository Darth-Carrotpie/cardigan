# What the heck is asynchronous communication anyway?

> Source: https://async.twist.com/asynchronous-communication/
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2022-05-17
> Author: Amir Salihefendic (Doist CEO), The Async Newsletter
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> asynchronous communication is when you send a message without expecting an immediate response.

> Asynchronous communication isn't primarily about tools

> about team culture and habits.

> Deep work becomes the default.

> Documentation and greater transparency become automatic.

> Synchronous communication should be the exception, not the rule.

> Set reasonable, team-wide expectations for acceptable response times.

> Promote writing and communication as core skills to master.

> Everyone needs to be a great writer in an async workplace.

> As with most things in life, async culture has pros and cons.

> It's hard to build rapport and personal relationships with only written communication.

> an uphill battle that challenges the status quo.

## Collector's note

Doist's CEO explains the async model used to run the company behind Todoist and Twist. The page (seen through a summarizer, so only short quotes are kept) also says Doist expects replies within 24 hours, reserves sync for 1-on-1s, critical feedback and sensitive topics, brainstorming under high uncertainty, and urgent crises, and that after cutting most meetings it had to bring some sync back because it "lost the human element". For cardigan this marks where async-first stops: sensitive feedback, rapport and crises are cases where the skill should not push a written message over a call.
