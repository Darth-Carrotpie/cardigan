# Shopify deleted 322,000 hours of meetings. Should the rest of us be jealous?

> Source: https://www.npr.org/2023/02/15/1156804295/shopify-delete-meetings-zoom-virtual-productivity
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2023-02-15
> Author: Andrea Hsu, NPR
> Type: journalism

## Excerpts

> In a January memo, the e-commerce platform called it "useful subtraction," a way to free up time to allow people to get stuff done.

> "We deleted 322,000 hours of meetings," Shopify's chief operating officer Kaz Nejatian proudly shared in a recent interview.

> That's in a company of about 10,000 employees, all remote.

> A bot went into everyone's calendars and purged all recurring meetings with three or more people, giving them that time back.

> Employees were told to wait two weeks before adding anything back to their calendars and to be "really, really critical" about what they bring back. Also, they have to steer clear of Wednesdays. Nejatian says 85% of employees are complying with their "No Meetings Wednesdays" policy.

> "People have been saying 'no' to meetings from me, and I'm the COO of the company. And that's great," he said.

> Microsoft found that the amount of time the average Teams user spent in meetings more than tripled between February 2020 and February 2022

> Steven Rogelberg, an organizational psychologist at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte, is emphatic that meetings are not in and of themselves the problem. Bad meetings are.

> the agenda items are all recycled, there are way more people than necessary in attendance, one person dominates, and they stretch on and on.

> "Meetings can be incredibly engaging, satisfying sources of inspiration and good decision making when they are conducted effectively," he said.

> Needless to say, Rogelberg is not a fan of the Shopify-style meeting purge.

## Collector's note

NPR's report one month into Shopify's January 2023 calendar purge. A bot deleted recurring meetings with three or more people, followed by a two-week cooling-off period and no-meeting Wednesdays. The COO reports 322,000 hours removed and that people are happier (self-reported, not independently measured). Meeting scientist Steven Rogelberg pushes back that the problem is bad meetings, not meetings. For cardigan it supports "question every recurring meeting / re-add only deliberately" as a default nudge, and Rogelberg's view argues for "fix the meeting (fewer attendees, real agenda)" as an option alongside "cancel it".
