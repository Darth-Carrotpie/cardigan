# Distributed Work's Five Levels of Autonomy

> Source: https://ma.tt/2020/04/Five-Levels-Of-Autonomy/
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2020-04-10
> Author: Matt Mullenweg (Automattic CEO)
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> "Level Zero" autonomy is a job which cannot be done unless you're physically there.

> there's no deliberate effort to make things remote-friendly.

> recreate what they were doing in the office in a "remote" setting.

> everything is still synchronous, your day is full of interruptions.

> you're really starting to benefit from being remote-first, or distributed.

> It's also the point at which you realize just how crucial written communication is for your success.

> you often also have a Google Doc up with the other meeting participants.

> You evaluate people's work on what they produce, not how or when they produce it.

> Trust emerges as the glue that holds the entire operation together.

> better — perhaps slower, but more deliberate — decision-making.

> Real-time meetings are respected and taken seriously, almost always have agendas.

> This is when you consistently perform better than any in-person organization could.

## Collector's note

Automattic's CEO lays out a maturity model for distributed companies. Level 2 copies office habits remotely ("everything is still synchronous"); levels 3 and 4 shift to written communication and asynchronous work. The fetch tool cut some quotes into partial sentences. For cardigan it gives a name to the anti-pattern "recreating the office online" (status meetings that could be written) and openly admits the cost that async decisions may be "slower, but more deliberate", a tradeoff the skill should show rather than hide when a decision is urgent.
