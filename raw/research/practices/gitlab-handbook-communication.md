## Excerpts

> To accomplish this, we use asynchronous communication as a starting point

> Asynchronous communication is the art of communicating and moving projects forward

> Sometimes synchronous communication is the better option, but do not default to it.

> The email or Slack message should not be the source of truth.

> The issue description should be the **single source of truth**.

> and say why, not just what.

> Ensure that every meeting has an agenda and is available for everyone to edit

> Meetings are incredibly expensive since they require synchronous time.

> While meetings recordings are helpful, written notes are more efficient to read

> written notes are more efficient to read and offer greater opportunities for async engagement

> Team members can add thoughts to an agenda in advance of a meeting and understand what was discussed if they cannot attend.

> You are not expected to be available all the time.

> There is no expectation to respond to messages outside of your planned working hours.

> Use low-context communications by being explicit in your communications

> Provide as much context as possible to avoid confusion.

> If you send a handbook link to someone as an answer to a question, consider adding some context

> please immediately make a merge request to add it to the handbook in a place you have looked for it

> leading by example to ensure that question only needs to be answered once

> Use issues or public chat channels (like `#questions`) instead of direct messages or one-on-one emails.

> Edit for brevity and clarity, especially in one-to-many communications.

> Keep it simple, brief and summarize what is important.

## Collector's note

GitLab's public handbook page on communication for its all-remote company. The rendered handbook page would not load past navigation, so excerpts come from the handbook's markdown source on gitlab.com, first ~100K of ~157K characters. For cardigan it supports several rules: async as the default, with sync allowed but not the default; one source of truth, so reply with a link rather than restating; "answer once, then write it down"; written notes over recordings; and, against plain terseness, "low-context" explicitness, adding context when replying with only a link, and "say why, not just what".
