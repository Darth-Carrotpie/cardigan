# Documenting Architecture Decisions

> Source: https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2011-11-15
> Author: Michael Nygard (Cognitect)
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> One of the hardest things to track during the life of a project is the motivation behind certain decisions.

> Without understanding the rationale or consequences, this person has only two choices:

> It's better to avoid either blind acceptance or blind reversal.

> These documents have names that are short noun phrases.

> This section describes the forces at play, including technological, political, social, and project local.

> It is stated in full sentences, with active voice.

> This section describes the resulting context, after applying the decision.

> The whole document should be one or two pages long.

> We will write each ADR as if it is a conversation with a future developer.

## Collector's note

The original post proposing Architecture Decision Records (ADRs): short records with Title, Context, Decision, Status and Consequences sections, one or two pages long. The post also says a decision may be marked "proposed" before stakeholders agree. It is a software practice, but it carries over to office "decision logs". For cardigan it backs a light-decision-record template (what we decided, why, what follows, status) over long minutes, and it argues that the rationale is the part you must not cut, since without it later readers can only accept or reverse blindly.
