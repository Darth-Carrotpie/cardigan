# Nine risks caused by AI notetakers

> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2025-11-20
> Author: Rachel Coldicutt / Careful Industries
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> Low-quality and/or incorrect outputs, due to transcription errors.

> Discriminatory outcomes for people with protected characteristics

> Declining quality of workplace culture.

> Increased day-to-day workload.

> Over-collection of personal data

> Inappropriately sharing confidential, proprietary or sensitive information

> AI transcription tools are not currently mature or reliable enough

> rely on someone checking the full transcript to ensure full accuracy

> unhappy or uncomfortable when automated notetaking was deployed as standard

> Best practice is to treat transcription in the same way as an audio-visual recording.

> Create a clear consent process

> human-created meeting summaries may well contain mistakes and biases

> rather than taking comprehensive notes

## Collector's note

Practitioner counterweight to the "auto meeting notes replace the recap email" claim: AI notes shift work to checking transcripts, create privacy/consent and disclosure burdens (subject access, FOI), and can chill candour. The author's lower-risk alternative is agreeing shared actions/headline summaries rather than comprehensive notes, and having fewer meetings. For cardigan, supports a caveat: prefer native auto-notes only with consent, and the laziest safe output is often a short agreed action list. Year inferred from the URL (page shows "20 Nov").
