## Collector's note

World Bank blog responding to its own Policy Research Working Paper 6851 (Doemeland and Trevino, 2014, "Which World Bank Reports Are Widely Read?"), which per search summaries examined 1,611 policy reports from 2008-2012 and found 31% were never downloaded and about 40% downloaded fewer than 100 times (the 40% and 1,611 figures were not in the fetched blog text). The blog argues download counts understate impact because many reports were written for a specific government counterpart, and that a survey found most led to policy changes. Relevance to cardigan: the best hard evidence located for "documents nobody reads", plus the important nuance that a report with a single intended reader can be valuable even if nobody else opens it. Suggests cardigan's test should be "does the intended reader need this?" rather than "will many people read this?"
