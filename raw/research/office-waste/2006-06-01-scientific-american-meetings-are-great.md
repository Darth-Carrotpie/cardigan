## Collector's note

Short report (SA Mind Vol. 17, No. 3) on Rogelberg and colleagues' questionnaire study of 980 workers relating time in scheduled meetings to job satisfaction. Per the article (paraphrased), goal-oriented workers whose jobs need little outside input are dissatisfied with meetings, while workers whose jobs depend on interaction with others and are flexible/unstructured are more satisfied the more meetings they attend. Relevance to cardigan: opposing evidence to "meetings are waste": the value of a meeting depends on the attendee's task interdependence, and complaining about meetings is partly a social norm. Suggests cardigan should cut meetings for low-interdependence attendees (make them optional, send notes) rather than cut meetings uniformly. Old (2006) and a news summary, not the paper itself.
