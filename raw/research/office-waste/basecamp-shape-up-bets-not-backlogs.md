# Bets, Not Backlogs (Shape Up, Chapter 7)

> Source: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.1-chapter-07
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: Unknown
> Author: Ryan Singer (Basecamp)
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> Backlogs are a big weight we don't need to carry.

> Dozens and eventually hundreds of tasks pile up that we all know we'll never have time for.

> The growing pile gives us a feeling like we're always behind even though we're not.

> The time spent constantly reviewing, grooming and organizing old ideas prevents everyone from moving forward.

> Really important ideas will come back to you.

> If you hear it once and never again, maybe it wasn't really a problem.

> Everyone can still track pitches, bugs, requests, or things they want to do independently without a central backlog.

> If we don't, we let it go. There's nothing we need to track or hold on to.

> Before each six-week cycle, we hold a betting table where stakeholders decide what to do in the next cycle.

> With just a few options and a six-week long cycle, these meetings are infrequent, short, and intensely productive.

## Collector's note

Chapter of Basecamp's Shape Up methodology (the book was released in 2019; the chapter page carries no date) arguing against maintained backlogs and grooming rituals in favour of a short, infrequent "betting table" before each six-week cycle. Relevance to cardigan: practitioner case for cutting planning rituals and tracking overhead ("work about work"): ideas that matter recur, so not every request needs logging, grooming or a planning meeting. Applies to planning/backlog output, less to commitments already made (those still need an owner and date).
