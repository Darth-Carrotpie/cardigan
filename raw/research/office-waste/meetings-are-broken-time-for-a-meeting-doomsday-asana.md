# Meetings are broken. It's time for a meeting doomsday.

> Source: https://asana.com/resources/meetings-broken-doomsday
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: Unknown
> Author: Rebecca Hinds (Asana Work Innovation Lab; originally published in Inc.)
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> I worked with one of our internal teams to cleanse employees' calendars of meetings that had become less productive over time.

> Each employee evaluates their recurring meetings and identifies which ones lack value.

> have your team delete all their recurring meetings from their calendars.

> After deleting their meetings, have your employees sit with their newly cleansed calendars for 48 hours.

> repopulate their calendars, but only with those meetings that are valuable

> Did the meeting help advance your work forward more quickly than you would have been able to do asynchronously?

> participants saved an average of 11 hours per month

> each individual saving 17 workdays over the course of a year.

> most of these meetings were once productive but had grown in size or scope and needed to be reevaluated.

> they changed many of their meetings to be shorter, unconventional lengths (like 15 minutes).

> They also changed the cadence of meetings to be less frequent.

> Employees often feel that if they delete meetings, other attendees will take it as a personal affront.

## Collector's note

Practitioner write-up of Asana's internal "Meeting Doomsday" (one-month experiment): delete all recurring meetings, wait 48 hours, re-add only those that beat async. The Asana page displays "August 10th, 2026" (apparently a repost/update date) and says it was originally published in Inc. with no date, so the original date is unknown. No counts of meetings deleted or re-added are reported, and no negative results. Relevance to cardigan: a concrete, repeatable audit rule ("does this beat async?") and the finding that meetings decay by growing in size/scope, so the fix is often shrink/shorten/de-cadence rather than delete. Note the Fortune 2026 piece reports "up to 11 hours per week" for Hinds-led doomsdays versus "11 hours per month" here.
