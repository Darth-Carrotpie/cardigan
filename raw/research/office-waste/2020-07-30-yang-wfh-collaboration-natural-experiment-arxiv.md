# How Work From Home Affects Collaboration: A Large-Scale Study of Information Workers in a Natural Experiment During COVID-19

> Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.15584
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2020-07-30
> Author: Longqi Yang, Sonia Jaffe, David Holtz, Siddharth Suri, Shilpi Sinha, Jeffrey Weston, Connor Joyce, Neha Shah, Kevin Sherman, CJ Lee, Brent Hecht, Jaime Teevan (Microsoft)
> Type: study

## Excerpts

> We use difference-in-differences (DiD), a causal identification strategy commonly used in the social sciences,

> to control for unobserved confounding factors and estimate the causal effect of WFH.

> Our analysis relies on measuring the difference in changes between those who WFH prior to COVID-19 and those who did not.

> The change attributable to WFH specifically is in the opposite direction: less time on collaboration and more focus time.

> This reversal shows the importance of using causal inference: a simple analysis would have resulted in the wrong conclusion.

> Meanwhile, the medium for collaboration has also shifted due to WFH:

> instant messages were used more, whereas scheduled meetings were used less.

## Collector's note

Microsoft Research preprint (later published in Nature Human Behaviour, September 2021, as "The effects of remote work on collaboration among information workers", 61,182 US Microsoft employees). Microsoft's summary of the published version (paraphrased, not quoted) reports cross-group collaboration time fell about 25%, networks became more static and siloed, meeting/call hours fell about 5% while async email and IM rose, and the authors warn this may make it harder to share new information across the network. A related secondary summary of the preprint's body cites meeting hours down 6.43% and message hours up 27.71% (not verified against the PDF). Relevance to cardigan: opposing/cautionary evidence: when synchronous meetings shrink, the volume often shifts to chat rather than disappearing, and cross-team information flow can suffer. Cardigan's "skip the meeting" advice should not just convert one meeting into a dozen messages, and should protect cross-team touchpoints.
