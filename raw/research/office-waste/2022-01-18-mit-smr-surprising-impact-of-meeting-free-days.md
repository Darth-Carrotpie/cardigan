# The Surprising Impact of Meeting-Free Days

> Source: https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-surprising-impact-of-meeting-free-days/
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2022-01-18
> Author: Benjamin Laker, Vijay Pereira, Pawan Budhwar, Ashish Malik (MIT Sloan Management Review)
> Type: study

## Excerpts

> We recently surveyed 76 companies, with more than 1,000 employees each and operations in more than 50 countries,

> that had introduced from one to five no-meeting days per week (prohibiting even one-on-one meetings)

> we spoke with managers and each company's HR director to obtain executive perspectives on the approaches taken

> assessed the subsequent impact on productivity, collaboration, and engagement, using pulse surveys.

> Nearly half (47%) of the companies we studied reduced meetings by 40% by introducing two no-meeting days per week.

> The remaining companies attempted something even more ambitious: 35% instituted three no-meeting days

> 11% implemented four.

> The remaining 7% eradicated meetings entirely.

> When one no-meeting day per week was introduced, autonomy, communication, engagement, and satisfaction all improved

> resulting in decreased micromanagement and stress, which caused productivity to rise.

> But arguably, the best results were achieved at companies that had three meeting-free days per week.

## Collector's note

Survey of 76 large companies that adopted one to five no-meeting days; outcomes measured via employee pulse surveys and stress data before/after, so results are self-reported and the companies self-selected (not randomized). Widely cited secondary figures (71% productivity rise, etc.) come from a table not captured in the fetched text, so they are not quoted here. Relevance to cardigan: the strongest commonly cited evidence that structurally removing meetings helps, but with the caveat (raised by the authors and practitioners elsewhere) that zero meetings is not the optimum and meetings can spill into other days.
