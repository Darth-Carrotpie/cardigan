# LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations

> Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076 (HTML v1: https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076v1)
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2024-04-15
> Author: Arjun Panickssery (MATS), Samuel R. Bowman (NYU, Anthropic), Shi Feng (NYU)
> Type: study

## Excerpts

> But new biases are introduced due to the same LLM acting as both the evaluator and the evaluatee.

> One such bias is self-preference, where an LLM evaluator scores its own outputs higher than others' while human annotators consider them of equal quality.

> We discover that, out of the box, LLMs such as GPT-4 and Llama 2 have non-trivial accuracy at distinguishing themselves from other LLMs and humans.

> By fine-tuning LLMs, we discover a linear correlation between self-recognition capability and the strength of self-preference bias; using controlled experiments, we show that the causal explanation resists straightforward confounders.

> In self-evaluation, as the name suggests, the same underlying LLM acts as both the evaluator and the evaluatee. As a result, the neutrality of the evaluator is in question, and the evaluation can suffer from biases where the LLM evaluators diverge from humans in systematic ways

> Frontier LLMs exhibit self-preference in self-evaluation. On two summarization tasks, LLMs (GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4, and Llama 2) disproportionately favor summaries written by themselves over those by other LLMs and from humans.

> LLMs have non-trivial self-recognition capability out of the box. All three LLMs we evaluate achieve over 50% accuracy at distinguishing their own outputs from other sources using simple prompts without fine-tuning. GPT-4 is 73.5% accurate distinguishing itself from two other LLMs and humans.

> Fine-tuning leads to near-perfect self-recognition.

## Collector's note

Summarization experiments (CNN/DailyMail, XSUM) showing LLM judges prefer their own outputs and that this tracks their ability to recognise their own text. In cardigan's evals both arms are generated by the same model, so self-preference does not favour one arm directly — but a judge from the same family may recognise "house style" and stylistic tells (e.g. cardigan's terse register) can unblind it. Prefer a judge from a different model family or at least a cross-check with one, plus human spot-checks.
