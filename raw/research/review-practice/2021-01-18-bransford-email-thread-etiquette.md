# We need to talk about email thread etiquette

> Source: https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2021/01/we-need-to-talk-about-email-thread-etiquette
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2021-01-18
> Author: Nathan Bransford (author, former literary agent at Curtis Brown Ltd.); final excerpt is from a reader comment
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> It's very important to remember that you are one out of several hundred people an agent/publishing professional is talking to at any given time. They're not going to remember every single detail of your previous correspondence.

> This gets us to Email Thread Principle #1: Include all of your previous correspondence when emailing a publishing professional

> If your previous correspondence is in the same thread it's easy for the publishing professional to dip back into the old emails to refresh their memory, to find past documents, and to have everything in one place.

> Email thread Principle #3: Do not change the subject line no matter how insane it is.

> If you change the subject line: Gmail treats it like a completely new conversation and it breaks the thread, making it much more difficult to find previous correspondence and documents.

> But again, remember: they're probably corresponding with way more people than you are. Defer to their approach.

Reader comment on the post (not the author):

> One of my pet peeves is when someone forwards me a long thread and doesn't summarize or state what is needed from me. I have received 6 month+ long threads that I was never copied on and simply asked, please assist. [...] Whenever I forward an email thread, I always summarize the situation and specifically ask what is needed from recipient.

## Collector's note

A former literary agent argues that busy recipients rely on the thread for context, so senders should reply in the thread, keep the subject line, and follow the recipient's conventions. A reader comment adds the complementary rule for forwarding: summarise and state the ask rather than sending a bare thread. For cardigan, this supports reading and keeping the thread as context, and adding a one-line summary plus the ask when forwarding. The source is aimed at publishing, not corporate email.
