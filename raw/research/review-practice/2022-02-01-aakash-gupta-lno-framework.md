# LNO Framework for Product Managers

> Source: https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/lno-framework-for-product-managers
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2022-02-01
> Author: Aakash Gupta (product leader), expanding Shreyas Doshi's LNO thread of Feb 1, 2020
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> As he framed it, "You can increase your impact on the organization (and reduce the tremendous stress that often accompanies the PM job) by avoiding the trap of doing a great job on all tasks (and even features). Seek leverage, actively try to do a bad job for certain tasks."

> Leverage tasks are those that 10x your impact. These are the types of tasks, once you identify, you should focus on. On the other hand, Neutral tasks are the kind that offer regular impact in exchange for time. Finally, overhead tasks are the type that have very little impact for the time they take.

> Feel free to keep iterating on the work, driving towards perfection. You want the A+. These are the tasks through which you will move the world.

> This work has to be done, but PMs do not need to edit grammar on slack messages. [...] Because these types of tasks demand attention, it is important to recognize these and understand that you are looking for just good enough. The easiest mistake of frazzled PMs using LNO is to consider neutral work leveraged, because of their perfectionist natures.

> Aim for C+/B- when encountering these types of tasks.

> If everyone is pouring their heart and soul into an Overhead task and you aren't, the pressure to conform will be real.

> Overhead work is the type of work you need to actively try to do a bad job. Because you are a PM, your bad job will be good enough. Just get the work done.

## Collector's note

A product leader's expansion of Shreyas Doshi's LNO (Leverage / Neutral / Overhead) framework, quoting Doshi's original thread ("actively try to do a bad job for certain tasks"). It recommends polishing leverage work to A+, aiming for C+/B- on neutral work such as Slack messages, and doing overhead work in the minimum way. This is the opposing view to heavy review: cardigan should classify an item's stakes before deciding how much polish or review to give it, and should not spend editing effort on low-leverage messages. Doshi's original thread was not fetched; his words come through Gupta.
