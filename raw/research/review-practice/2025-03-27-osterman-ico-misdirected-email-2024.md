# Misdirected communications – 2024 update from the ICO

> Source: https://ostermanresearch.com/2025/03/27/misdirected-communications-2024-update-from-the-ico
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2025-03-27
> Author: Osterman Research
> Type: vendor-data

## Excerpts

> The most common data security incident reported to the Information Commissioner's Office (UK) for October to December 2024 was … unsurprisingly, misdirected emails. The frequency of using email for communicating with others, the ease of stumbling when using type-ahead addressing in Outlook and other email clients, and the frenetic pace of much office work means that it's just too easy to choose the wrong person. Of the total incident count reported to the ICO, 21% were of this type.

> There should also be a necessary emphasis on training users to check and double check when adding someone to an email message or distribution list, but that's not guaranteed to work in all instances.

> The cost of getting it wrong is reputational mainly, although the extent of that cost and ancillary costs will depend enormously on the contents of the misdirected communication. Banal stuff … not so much. Corporate IP, confidential data, and data subject to privacy regulations … much more so. Excel spreadsheets with customer information – yes, that's a problem.

> The higher the risk, the more layered a mitigation approach should be.

## Collector's note

An analyst firm's short summary of UK ICO incident data: misdirected email was the most common data security incident reported in Q4 2024, at 21% of incidents. The firm blames type-ahead addressing and the pace of office work, and says the effort spent on checking should rise with how sensitive the content is. This is secondhand reporting of ICO figures; the ICO's own dashboard was not fetched. For cardigan, it supports making the recipient check a pre-send step that can't be skipped, scaled up when an email carries confidential or personal data or an attachment.
