## Excerpts

> Day 2 companies make high-quality decisions, but they make high-quality decisions slowly.

> To keep the energy and dynamism of Day 1, you have to somehow make high-quality, high-velocity decisions.

> most decisions should probably be made with somewhere around 70% of the information you wish you had.

> Third, use the phrase "disagree and commit." This phrase will save a lot of time.

> They had a completely different opinion and wanted to go ahead. I wrote back right away with "I disagree and commit and hope it becomes the most watched thing we've ever made."

## Collector's note

Bezos's 2016 shareholder letter (the page gives no exact date; these letters normally come out in April of the following year) introduces "disagree and commit" as a way to cap how long a disagreement runs: say your view once, then fully back the decision. This is the counterweight to pushback, i.e. once an objection is on record and the decision-maker has chosen, keep executing rather than reopening it. For cardigan: after one flagged question or lighter alternative, if the requester still wants the full deliverable, produce it without relitigating. The "70% of the information" line also supports proceeding on a stated assumption instead of waiting for clarification.
