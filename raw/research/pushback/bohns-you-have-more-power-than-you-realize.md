# You Have More Power Over Others Than You Realize

> Source: https://evidencebasedliving.human.cornell.edu/blog/you-have-more-power-over-others-than-you-realize
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: Unknown
> Author: Evidence Based Living blog, Cornell University (quoting social psychologist Vanessa Bohns)
> Type: study

## Excerpts

> we underestimate how difficult it is for people to say no to us.

> When we are on the receiving end of a request, we know how incredibly awkward and uncomfortable it is to say no,

> We worry about hurting someone's feelings

> people often feel pressured to agree to do things they don't necessarily feel comfortable with

> participants only needed to ask six people.

> about half of the strangers who were approached agreed to let study participants use their phones.

> people may feel less comfortable rejecting our romantic advances and unethical requests than we think.

> when talking to people face-to-face, we tend to trust that what they are telling us is true.

> Over e-mail, we need to work harder to establish trust and get people's attention.

## Collector's note

A Cornell outreach post summarising Vanessa Bohns's "underestimation of compliance" research. In the phone-borrowing study participants expected to ask about 10 strangers to get 3 yeses and needed only about 6. Her reviews report that requesters underestimate compliance by about 48% on average, because refusing feels awkward and socially costly. For cardigan this cuts two ways. Users find it hard to say no to requests, so they over-produce. And when cardigan has the user ask a manager or client "do you still need X?", the other person may also find it hard to say "yes, I do", so the question should be easy to answer either way and come with the deliverable already on offer.
