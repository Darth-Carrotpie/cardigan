## Collector's note

An interview with Edmondson and Detert about their early fieldwork on employee silence, the research that led to their 2011 AMJ paper "Implicit Voice Theories" (190 interviews plus a 265-person survey, per secondary sources). They argue that silence comes from fear of offending people above you, not from disengagement, and that people speak up only when it feels both safe and worthwhile. For cardigan: users will often default to silent compliance with a manager's request, so a skill that nudges them to question the deliverable is working against a strong, natural self-censorship reflex. The nudge should be cheap and low-risk, such as a one-line question with the work already attached.
