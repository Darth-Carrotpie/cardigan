## Collector's note

NYU Stern's research summary of the 2005 JPSP paper, which tested senders' confidence that email recipients would correctly read sarcasm, seriousness, anger, sadness or humour. Misreadings were common even between people who knew each other, and senders didn't notice; the authors put this down to egocentrism. Secondary coverage (APA Monitor, 2006) reports roughly 56% email accuracy against about 90% self-rated accuracy, but this page gives no numbers. For cardigan: a one-line pushback or a stripped-down reply may read colder in the inbox than the writer intends, so a short reply needs explicit tone signals (an acknowledgment, a reason) and shouldn't rely on implied goodwill.
