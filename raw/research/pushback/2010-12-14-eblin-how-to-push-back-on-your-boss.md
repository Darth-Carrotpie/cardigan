# How to Push Back on Your Boss

> Source: https://govexec.com/management/2010/12/how-to-push-back-on-your-boss/40296
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: 2010-12-14
> Author: Scott Eblin (executive coach), Government Executive
> Type: practitioner

## Excerpts

> Do your best to understand the goal or motivation behind the boss's request.

> If you understand the true motivation, you're in a better position to offer alternative courses of action.

> Explain how one or more of those alternatives will accomplish the goal as well as if not better than the request.

> If you want to be heard, you're going to have to keep it short enough to keep their attention.

> [...] they're moving at 350 miles per hour and haven't stopped to consider the impact of what they're asking for.

> Use your holds judiciously and save them for big things.

> Fold on the little things.

> [...] you'll eventually get shut out of the decision making process.

## Collector's note

Executive coach Scott Eblin's playbook for pushing back on a boss: find the goal behind the request, offer alternatives that meet that goal as well or better, keep it short, and save pushback for things that matter, because constant challenge gets you excluded from decisions. He also says that after a couple of refused pushbacks you have to decide whether to keep going or drop it. For cardigan: anchor any lighter version to the requester's underlying goal, don't question small requests at all, and budget pushback per relationship rather than applying it to every deliverable. (The elided subject of the "350 miles per hour" sentence is bosses.)
