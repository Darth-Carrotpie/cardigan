# The Risks and Rewards of Speaking Up: Managerial Responses to Employee Voice

> Source: https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W2039335540
> Collected: 2026-10-31
> Published: Unknown
> Author: Ethan R. Burris (Academy of Management Journal 55(4), 2012)
> Type: study

## Excerpts

> This article examines whether managerial responses to employees speaking up depend on the type of voice exhibited

> In one field study and two experimental studies, I found that managers view employees who engage in more challenging forms of voice as worse performers [...] endorse their ideas less than those who engage in supportive forms of voice.

> Further, perceptions of loyalty and threat mediated these relationships, but in different ways.

> I discuss implications for research on voice, proactivity, and social persuasion.

## Collector's note

Abstract of Burris (AMJ 2012). Secondary summaries describe a field study of 281 restaurant managers and their supervisors plus experiments with MBA and EMBA students and working professionals. Managers rated "challenging" voice (aimed at changing current practice) as worse performance and endorsed those ideas less than "supportive" voice, because they saw it as less loyal and more threatening. This is the strongest evidence here for the social cost of pushback. For cardigan: questioning whether a manager's requested deliverable should exist is challenging voice, so frame it as support for the manager's goal ("to get you X faster, here's a lighter version") rather than as a critique of the request.
