# Message overload

> Sources: Longqi Yang et al. / Microsoft, 2020-07-30; Microsoft WorkLab, 2025-06-17
> Raw: [Breaking Down the Infinite Workday](../../raw/research/office-waste/2025-06-17-microsoft-wti-breaking-down-infinite-workday.md); [How Work From Home Affects Collaboration](../../raw/research/office-waste/2020-07-30-yang-wfh-collaboration-natural-experiment-arxiv.md)
> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

Knowledge workers receive more messages than they can read with care. Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index special report, built on Microsoft 365 telemetry and a large survey, puts the average at 117 emails a day, most skimmed in under a minute, plus 153 Teams messages, with an interruption every two minutes. Every extra message competes for that skim. The figures are vendor data from a company that also sells the AI remedy.

## The volume

Microsoft WorkLab's report (vendor-data; "All data is based on aggregated and anonymized Microsoft 365 productivity signals, ending February 15, 2025.", plus a survey "among 31,000 full-time employed or self-employed knowledge workers across 31 markets") reports:

- "The average worker receives 117 emails daily—most of them skimmed in under 60 seconds."
- "The average worker receives 153 Teams messages per weekday."
- "Messages per person are up 6% YOY globally"
- "Employees are interrupted every two minutes during core work hours—275 times a day—by meetings, emails, or chats."

Work spills past the day: "the average employee now sends or receives more than 50 messages outside of core business hours", and "by 10 pm, nearly a third (29%) of active workers dive back into their inboxes".

## Meetings without invites

The report says "57% of meetings are ad hoc calls without a calendar invite" in its body and "60% of meetings are unscheduled or ad hoc." in its methodology; the two figures are not reconciled on the page. Scheduled meetings cluster: "Half (50%) of all meetings take place between 9–11 am and 1–3 pm". Deck work bunches up right before meetings: "in the final 10 minutes before a meeting, PowerPoint edits spike 122%" (see [Slide decks](slide-decks.md)).

## The report's remedy

The report's advice is to "Follow the 80/20 rule." and to remember that "activity is not the same as progress". The rest of its prescription leans on Microsoft's own AI products, so it is not neutral.

## Fewer meetings, more messages

Messages and meetings trade off. Microsoft's earlier natural-experiment preprint (study) found that under work-from-home "instant messages were used more, whereas scheduled meetings were used less." Cutting one channel tends to fill another. See [Async-first communication](../practices/async-first.md).

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- The reader's budget is a skim of under a minute. cardigan puts the ask and deadline in the first line (see [BLUF](../practices/bluf.md)) because the rest may not be read.
- Sending nothing, or batching several points into one message, reduces load more than shortening each message does. The ladder's "no message" rung matters most in high-volume channels.
- cardigan should not add to after-hours volume: a draft written late can be scheduled for the recipient's working hours (a built-in send-later feature) rather than sent now.
- These are vendor-reported averages; cardigan's docs can use them to motivate the rules, not as evidence that a given rule improves outcomes.
