# Workslop

> Sources: Anthony Ha / TechCrunch, 2025-09-27
> Raw: [Beware co-workers who produce AI-generated 'workslop'](../../raw/research/office-waste/2025-09-27-techcrunch-ai-generated-workslop.md)
> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

"Workslop" is AI-generated work that looks finished but does not move the task forward, so the recipient has to interpret, fix or redo it. Researchers from BetterUp Labs and the Stanford Social Media Lab, writing in Harvard Business Review and reported by TechCrunch, found that many US employees had received some recently. For an AI writing agent it is the central risk: polished, padded output that shifts cost to the reader.

## Definition and cost

TechCrunch (journalism, reporting the HBR research by Niederhoffer, Kellerman, Lee, Liebscher, Rapuano and Hancock) quotes the definition: "AI generated work content that masquerades as good work, but lacks the substance to meaningfully advance a given task." Such content is "unhelpful, incomplete, or missing crucial context,".

The harm is where the work lands: "The insidious effect of workslop is that it shifts the burden of the work downstream", "requiring the receiver to interpret, correct, or redo the work."

## How common

The evidence is a survey (study, reported second-hand; the HBR original was paywalled when collected): an "ongoing survey of 1,150 full-time, U.S.-based employees", with "40% of respondents saying they'd received workslop in the past month." Figures on rework time per instance appear only in the collector's note and are not cited here.

## The researchers' advice

The recommendations are aimed at leaders: "model thoughtful AI use that has purpose and intention" and "set clear guardrails for your teams around norms and acceptable use."

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- cardigan's whole premise is the anti-workslop stance: produce less and denser, never pad to look thorough. Length, headings and bullet structure are not evidence of work.
- Missing context is one of the named failure modes, so cutting must never remove the context the reader needs to act; this backs the never-cut list (facts, owners, deadlines, the actual ask).
- A good eval for cardigan checks whether the recipient could act on the output without follow-up, not whether it reads as polished.
- When cardigan cannot produce substance (missing facts, unclear ask), the right output is a question or a short note saying what is missing, not a filled-in template.
