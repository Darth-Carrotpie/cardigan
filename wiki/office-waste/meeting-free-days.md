# Meeting-free days

> Sources: Benjamin Laker et al. / MIT Sloan Management Review, 2022-01-18; Natasha Piñon / CNBC, 2023-02-01
> Raw: [The Surprising Impact of Meeting-Free Days](../../raw/research/office-waste/2022-01-18-mit-smr-surprising-impact-of-meeting-free-days.md); [Meetings Aren't 'the Enemy'](../../raw/research/office-waste/2023-02-01-cnbc-canva-no-meeting-wednesdays-lessons.md)
> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

A meeting-free day bans meetings on fixed weekdays instead of deleting meetings one by one. A survey of large companies that adopted one to five such days reports better autonomy, engagement and productivity, with the best results at three days a week. Canva's head of people, after three years of no-meeting Wednesdays, warns of the failure mode: meetings displaced from the protected day pile onto the others, so a meeting-free day without pruning can make overload worse.

## The survey evidence

Laker, Pereira, Budhwar and Malik (study; MIT Sloan Management Review) "surveyed 76 companies, with more than 1,000 employees each and operations in more than 50 countries," "that had introduced from one to five no-meeting days per week (prohibiting even one-on-one meetings)". They interviewed managers and HR directors and "assessed the subsequent impact on productivity, collaboration, and engagement, using pulse surveys." The companies chose their own policies and the outcomes are survey-based, so this is observational evidence, not a controlled trial.

How far companies went:

- "Nearly half (47%) of the companies we studied reduced meetings by 40% by introducing two no-meeting days per week."
- "35% instituted three no-meeting days"
- "11% implemented four."
- "The remaining 7% eradicated meetings entirely."

Reported effects: "When one no-meeting day per week was introduced, autonomy, communication, engagement, and satisfaction all improved", "resulting in decreased micromanagement and stress, which caused productivity to rise." And: "the best results were achieved at companies that had three meeting-free days per week." The widely repeated percentage gains come from a table not captured in the raw note, so they are not cited here. The best result being three days, not five, implies the authors did not find zero meetings optimal.

## The displacement problem

Jennie Rogerson, Canva's global head of people (practitioner testimony relayed by CNBC journalism, not measured data), describes the starting point as calendars like "a massive game of Tetris, where they didn't have breathing space,". Her diagnosis is that the day is not the fix: "The real problem is too many pointless meetings". The failure mode: "If all your Wednesday meetings migrate to your already-busy Thursdays, you've arguably made the problem worse". Her rule: "Meetings should be really high quality. Every meeting should have a goal." And the framing: "Let's be really intentional about our time, versus just seeing meetings as the enemy,".

> **Status: Disputed**
> Laker et al. (MIT Sloan Management Review, 2022; survey of 76 companies) report that meeting-free days improved autonomy, engagement and productivity, with "the best results" at three days a week. Rogerson (Canva, via CNBC, 2023; practitioner testimony) warns that without pruning, meetings "migrate to your already-busy Thursdays" and the policy can make "the problem worse". The survey measures self-reported outcomes at companies that chose the policy; the testimony describes a mechanism the survey's design would not necessarily catch. Both agree the target is fewer pointless meetings, not zero meetings.

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- A protected day is a calendar feature (a recurring block, a team norm), not something cardigan writes. When a user asks for focus time, cardigan can suggest the built-in focus-time or recurring-block feature instead of drafting a policy memo.
- Moving a meeting is not cutting it. When cardigan proposes rescheduling around a no-meeting day, it should first ask whether the meeting needs to happen at all (see [Meeting purges](meeting-purges.md)).
- Every meeting cardigan helps schedule gets a one-line goal; a meeting without one is a candidate for a written update instead.
- Evidence strength matters for cardigan's docs: the 76-company survey is self-reported and self-selected, so it supports *worth trying*, not *proven to raise productivity*.
