# Backlogs

> Sources: Ryan Singer / Basecamp, undated (book 2019)
> Raw: [Bets, Not Backlogs (Shape Up, Chapter 7)](../../raw/research/office-waste/basecamp-shape-up-bets-not-backlogs.md)
> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

A maintained backlog of every idea and request is overhead: it grows faster than anyone can work it, makes the team feel behind, and eats time in grooming meetings. Basecamp's Shape Up replaces it with a short, infrequent "betting table" before each six-week cycle and lets unchosen ideas go, on the theory that important ones come back. The source is a practitioner method book describing Basecamp's own process.

## The case against the backlog

Ryan Singer (practitioner; Basecamp's Shape Up) is blunt: "Backlogs are a big weight we don't need to carry." "Dozens and eventually hundreds of tasks pile up that we all know we'll never have time for." The pile distorts how the team feels ("The growing pile gives us a feeling like we're always behind even though we're not.") and costs time: "The time spent constantly reviewing, grooming and organizing old ideas prevents everyone from moving forward."

## Let ideas come back

The replacement rests on recurrence: "Really important ideas will come back to you." "If you hear it once and never again, maybe it wasn't really a problem." Tracking is not banned, only centralised grooming: "Everyone can still track pitches, bugs, requests, or things they want to do independently without a central backlog." Unchosen work is dropped: "If we don't, we let it go. There's nothing we need to track or hold on to."

## The betting table

Planning becomes one short meeting per cycle: "Before each six-week cycle, we hold a betting table where stakeholders decide what to do in the next cycle." Because the inputs are few and the cycle long, "these meetings are infrequent, short, and intensely productive."

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- Not every request needs a ticket. When a user receives an idea or minor request with no commitment attached, cardigan's default can be a polite acknowledgement, not a new backlog item or a planning-meeting slot.
- Grooming rituals are a target for cutting like status meetings: cardigan can propose one short decision session per cycle instead of recurring backlog review.
- The never-cut list still wins: anything already promised to someone (with an owner or a date) is a commitment, not a backlog idea, and cardigan keeps it tracked.
- *Ideas come back* pairs with the default soft no in [Appetite](../practices/appetite.md): saying no now is cheap because the important ones will be raised again.
