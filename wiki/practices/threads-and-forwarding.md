# Threads and forwarding

> Sources: Nathan Bransford, 2021-01-18; Corrs Chambers Westgarth, 2021-04-21; Google (Gmail Help), undated
> Raw: [We need to talk about email thread etiquette](../../raw/research/review-practice/2021-01-18-bransford-email-thread-etiquette.md); [The increased danger of 'reply all': a warning for in-house counsel](../../raw/research/review-practice/2021-04-21-corrs-reply-all-danger-in-house-counsel.md); [Collaborate with Gemini in Gmail](../../raw/research/native-features/gemini-in-gmail.md)
> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

Busy recipients use the email thread as their memory, so replies should stay in the thread and keep its subject line. When a thread is forwarded to someone new, the forward needs a summary and a stated ask. The evidence is practitioner advice from publishing, plus a reader comment. A law firm's briefing pulls the other way for sensitive threads: start a new chain rather than forwarding a long one.

## Keep the thread

Nathan Bransford (practitioner; author and former literary agent, writing for authors who email agents) starts from the recipient's load: "you are one out of several hundred people an agent/publishing professional is talking to at any given time. They're not going to remember every single detail of your previous correspondence." Hence "Email Thread Principle #1: Include all of your previous correspondence when emailing a publishing professional", because "If your previous correspondence is in the same thread it's easy for the publishing professional to dip back into the old emails to refresh their memory, to find past documents, and to have everything in one place."

The subject line holds the thread together: "Do not change the subject line no matter how insane it is." Changing it in Gmail "breaks the thread, making it much more difficult to find previous correspondence and documents." Where the recipient's habits differ, the busier party wins: "Defer to their approach."

## Forward with a summary and an ask

A reader comment on the post (not the author) names the opposite failure, a bare forward: "One of my pet peeves is when someone forwards me a long thread and doesn't summarize or state what is needed from me." The commenter's practice: "Whenever I forward an email thread, I always summarize the situation and specifically ask what is needed from recipient."

Mail clients now offer the summary half on the reader's side. Google's Gmail help (official-docs) says Gemini can "Synthesize long email threads into concise bullet points", on an eligible paid plan, and warns that "Gemini features may suggest inaccurate or inappropriate information." The ask still has to come from the sender. See [Catch-up summaries](../native-features/catch-up-summaries.md).

## When to start a new chain

Corrs Chambers Westgarth (practitioner; an Australian law firm) gives the counter-case. For threads carrying legal advice, it recommends people "consider starting a new email chain rather than forwarding lengthy chains to multiple colleagues", and warns that chains which drift from a legal purpose to a mixed one can lose privilege. See [Recipient check](../review-practice/recipient-check.md).

> **Status: Disputed**
> Bransford (2021, practitioner, publishing) says to "Include all of your previous correspondence" and never change the subject line, so the recipient has everything in one place. Corrs (2021, practitioner, legal) recommends "starting a new email chain rather than forwarding lengthy chains to multiple colleagues" when advice is involved. The conflict is mostly one of context: Bransford writes about one-to-one correspondence with a busy recipient, Corrs about circulating sensitive content to many people.

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- Reply in the existing thread and keep its subject line by default; a new thread or subject is a deliberate choice (new topic, new audience, sensitive content going to more people), not a cleanup.
- Never forward a long thread bare. cardigan adds a two- or three-line summary and the specific ask at the top, which is BLUF applied to forwarding.
- The thread is context cardigan should read before drafting, so a reply does not re-ask what the thread already answers.
