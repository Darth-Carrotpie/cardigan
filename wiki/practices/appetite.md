# Appetite (fixed time, variable scope)

> Sources: Ryan Singer / Basecamp, undated (book 2019); Jeff Bezos / Amazon, 2018-04-18
> Raw: [Shape Up, Chapter 3: Set Boundaries](../../raw/research/practices/shape-up-set-boundaries-appetite.md); [Bets, Not Backlogs (Shape Up, Chapter 7)](../../raw/research/office-waste/basecamp-shape-up-bets-not-backlogs.md); [2017 Letter to Shareholders](../../raw/research/practices/2018-04-18-amazon-2017-shareholder-letter-six-page-memos.md)
> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

Appetite is how much time a piece of work is worth, decided before the work starts. Basecamp's Shape Up method fixes that time budget and cuts scope to fit, instead of estimating a fixed scope and letting time grow. It pairs this with a polite default "not now" for new ideas.

## Time budget first

Ryan Singer's Shape Up (practitioner; Basecamp's own product method) defines appetite as "The amount of time we want to spend on a project, as opposed to an estimate." It is "a time budget for a standard team size". Basecamp uses two sizes: a small batch that "a team of one designer and one or two programmers can build in one or two weeks", and a big batch that "takes the same-size team a full six-weeks."

The rule that follows: "This principle, called "fixed time, variable scope," is key to successfully defining and shipping projects." Quality is judged against the budget: "There's no absolute definition of "the best" solution. The best is relative to your constraints."

## The default answer to new ideas

Singer sets a soft no as the starting position: "Our default response to any idea that comes up should be: "Interesting. Maybe some day."" A later chapter gives the reason a soft no is safe: "Really important ideas will come back to you." Basecamp keeps no central backlog of declined ideas; see [Backlogs](../office-waste/backlogs.md).

## Relation to Amazon's memos

Bezos (see [Writing culture](writing-culture.md)) argues that people should be taught the real scope of a great memo, which takes "a week or more". This does not contradict Shape Up: both say decide the size of the effort up front. Bezos sets the budget high for high-stakes documents; Shape Up then cuts scope to fit whatever budget was chosen.

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- Before producing a document, plan or report, cardigan can state the appetite (a one-line reply, a one-page note, a half-day doc) and fit scope to it, rather than producing everything that could be said.
- When the work outgrows the appetite, cardigan cuts scope and says what it cut. It does not quietly stretch the output.
- "Interesting. Maybe some day." is a workable full reply to an unrequested idea; cardigan keeps it polite and can add one line of reason.
- Appetite never overrides the never-cut list: fixed time trims optional scope, not facts, owners, deadlines or compliance wording.
