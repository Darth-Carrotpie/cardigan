# One-on-ones

> Sources: Gallup, 2023-05-29; Khan Academy engineering, undated
> Raw: [A Great Manager's Most Important Habit](../../raw/research/office-waste/2023-05-29-gallup-great-managers-most-important-habit.md); [Khan/snippets (README)](../../raw/research/practices/khan-academy-weekly-snippets-readme.md)
> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

A short, frequent manager-employee conversation is the recurring meeting with the strongest case for keeping. Gallup's survey data associates weekly meaningful feedback with engagement and finds 15 to 30 minutes enough if the conversation happens regularly. In a calendar audit these look like recurring meetings, but the evidence points to shortening them, not deleting them. The evidence is correlational vendor data from a firm that sells management training.

## Short and frequent beats long and rare

Gallup (vendor-data; its own employee surveys) reports:

- "80% of employees who say they have received meaningful feedback in the past week are fully engaged"
- "the boost from meaningful feedback gives four times the lift in engagement"
- Few conversations land: "among nearly 15,000 employees, only 16% said the last conversation with their manager was extremely meaningful"

On length and cadence: "Between 15 and 30 minutes is enough time for a meaningful conversation, but only if it happens frequently." "In fact, 15- to 30-minute conversations have a greater impact than 30- to 60-minute conversations if they occur regularly." Skipping weeks costs time later: "if managers don't give employees feedback every week, they will need longer conversations to catch up."

The underlying studies "included 14,774 participants and 2,354 teams." The figures are associations from survey data, not causal estimates.

## Written status makes the meeting shorter

Khan Academy's snippets README (repo) lists as one purpose: "Managers can read snippets of their direct reports to make better use of 1-on-1 meetings". When status arrives in writing, the one-on-one can be spent on feedback rather than reporting. See [Written status updates](written-status-updates.md).

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- In a calendar cleanup, cardigan does not propose deleting one-on-ones. It may propose shortening a 60-minute one to 15–30 minutes and keeping it weekly.
- A one-on-one agenda drafted by cardigan leads with feedback and the employee's topics; status goes in a written update read beforehand.
- Feedback is relationship-heavy and sensitive, so cardigan does not suggest replacing a one-on-one with a message (see [Async-first communication](async-first.md) on rapport).
- Gallup's figures are vendor survey correlations; cardigan's docs should cite them as such, not as proof that one-on-ones cause engagement.
