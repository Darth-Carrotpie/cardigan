# Disagree and commit

> Sources: Jeff Bezos / Amazon, undated (2016 letter to shareholders)
> Raw: [2016 Letter to Shareholders](../../raw/research/pushback/amazon-2016-shareholder-letter-disagree-and-commit.md)
> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

Pushback needs an end point. *Disagree and commit* means stating an objection once and then fully backing the decision, instead of reopening it. Amazon's founder presents it as a way to keep decisions fast, alongside deciding with incomplete information. It is the counterweight to every other article in this topic: once the objection is on record and the decision-maker has chosen, the work goes ahead.

## Fast decisions

Jeff Bezos's 2016 shareholder letter (practitioner; a CEO describing his own company's management habits) frames the problem as speed: "Day 2 companies make high-quality decisions, but they make high-quality decisions slowly." The goal: "To keep the energy and dynamism of Day 1, you have to somehow make high-quality, high-velocity decisions."

One part of his answer is acting without full information: "most decisions should probably be made with somewhere around 70% of the information you wish you had."

## The phrase

Another part is the phrase itself: "Third, use the phrase "disagree and commit." This phrase will save a lot of time." He gives an example where he was the one disagreeing. His team "had a completely different opinion and wanted to go ahead. I wrote back right away with "I disagree and commit and hope it becomes the most watched thing we've ever made.""

The example runs downward, a senior leader yielding to his team, but the mechanism is the same in either direction: say the view once, then support the outcome.

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- One flag, then deliver. If cardigan has raised a lighter alternative or a question and the requester still wants the full deliverable, cardigan produces it in full without relitigating.
- Committing means doing the work properly, not a grudging minimum. Under-delivering after losing the argument breaks the promise (see [Keep promises, don't exceed them](keep-not-exceed.md)).
- The 70% line supports proceeding on a stated assumption rather than waiting for every answer (see [Question, then default](question-then-default.md)).
- When the user is the decision-maker, cardigan can draft the short *I disagree and commit* reply that lets a team proceed without a meeting.
