# Accuracy checklists

> Updated: 2026-10-31

## Overview

A short, fixed list of the fact types most likely to be wrong (names, titles, places, dates, numbers, math, superlatives, quotes) lets a writer check their own work before anyone else sees it. Claims that cannot be verified are deleted or qualified and attributed. The evidence is newsroom practitioner advice, including NPR's checklist as reproduced by a trade association.

## Check the risky facts yourself

Poynter's roundup of copy editors' advice (practitioner) argues that fewer reviewers make self-checking essential: "Writers should use a personal accuracy checklist to review their work before passing it to an editor. The reality is newsrooms have fewer sets of eyes checking each piece of copy, so it's more essential than ever to be a great self editor."

Pam Nelson of ACES lists the items "editors (and writers!) need to check": "spelling of names and places, dates, numbers and math."

## NPR's checklist

The American Society of Business Publication Editors (practitioner; a trade association reproducing NPR's checklist) lists, among others:

- **Superlatives.** "If something is said to be the"first," "last," "best," "only," "oldest," "youngest" etc., that claim must be verified. If it can't be, the claim should be deleted or qualified — and clearly attributed."
- **Names and titles.** "Personal Names: Verify them, spell them correctly". "Titles: President, CEO, professor, etc. They must be accurate."
- **Locations.** "Is that where this happened? Is that where this person is from?"
- **Numbers.** "Do the math yourself. Should it be millions, billions or trillions? Is the decimal in the correct place? Is it percent or percentage point?"

ASBPE's conclusion: "matters pertaining to SUPERLATIVES and NUMBERS clearly deserve inclusion in any accuracy checklist you devise."

## Quotes that read oddly

A specific failure: a quote that says the opposite of what you'd expect may be a transcription error. "check the quote with the writer. Sometimes a writer drops the "not" in a quote."

## For cardigan

Interpretation, not source claims:

- Before cardigan sends or hands over any draft, it re-checks the never-cut items it carries: names, dates, numbers (including any arithmetic it did), owners and deadlines, against the source thread or document.
- Unverifiable superlatives ("first", "only", "best") in a draft get flagged: cut them, or qualify and attribute them. Numbers get recomputed, with units and percent versus percentage point checked.
- A short fixed checklist is cheap enough to run on every output, even when the rest of the review is skipped for low stakes.
- A sentence whose meaning flips on a negation is a check item; cardigan should confirm it rather than "fix" it.
